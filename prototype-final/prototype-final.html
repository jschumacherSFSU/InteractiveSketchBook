Interface System Statement

I built this prototype using a standard navigation model of top level menu linked to sub-menus for each of four sections of the system. I decided to reduce the number of initial access points the user would encounter to streamline the experience and help them understand the flow of the pages. For the mobile version, the navigation is located at the bottom of the screen as is often found on many mobile sites, and employs a common hamburger menu the user can tap to access the top-layer menu. From there, the user selects a sub-menu to see the second-layer menu of individual page links leading to the desired page. On each page the user can access the home menu through the hamburger menu and move forward and backward using the right/left chevrons (these return to the second -layer menu in the prototype.)

The hierarchy was established through some exploration of size based on appearance on the mobile template. There are unique styles for H1-H3 for mobile, H1-H3 for desktop, and a subset of cross-platform styles. The mobile screen required smaller font sizes than the desktop version, thus the platform-specific values.

I wanted a simple clean appearance for the system, so for the mobile I decided to use a simple white background and started with gray for the page title and the menu bar. This was later adjusted to purple after the pages were well established. For the menu animation, I played around with sliding in from the side and from the top, and based on feedback from a peer, decided on sliding in from the top. This is the consistent movement for all the menus. The desktop similarly has menus that slide in from the top under the top-level menu buttons.

As I designed the screens, I tried different approaches for the menus and interactions and arrived on using the hamburger menu because of its well-known use as the best signifier for the home menu. I felt the slide in was the best affordance for the menu and it was easy to establish a consistency as well. I chose the following heuristics as most important while designing the system: visibility of system status, consistency and standards and aesthetic and minimalist design. The system status was important given the multiple sections and many pages that exist in this system. A user could easily get confused without knowing where they are in relation to the top- and second-layer menus or other pages. In my opinion, consistency across pages and elements is very important. It provides the user with a sense of comfort knowing which elements perform a particular task or what the hierarchy definition means. While we were not meant to focus on aesthetics, I still chose to add this to my thought process early on to achieve my desired clean and simple format.

The menu structure aligns with this thinking as well with a simple top-level menu followed by the sub-menus that clearly define the pages available for viewing. The hierarchy also contributes to the structure by providing the use with clear points of demarcation from the H1 and down.
